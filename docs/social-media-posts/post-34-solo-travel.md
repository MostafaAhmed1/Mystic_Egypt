## Caption
The question assumes Egypt is somehow less welcoming than other places, or that solo travel is inherently lonely, or that the default state of a traveler is isolation. None of those are true in my experience, but especially not in Egypt.

The culture there is built around hospitality in a way that's hard to explain to people who haven't experienced it. You walk into a shop to buy water and end up talking to the owner about his daughter's university exams. You sit at a café and the waiter brings you a second tea you didn't order and says "on the house, you look tired." You ask for directions and someone walks you there.

The loneliness that people imagine is a projection, not a reality. The reality is people constantly checking on you, inviting you, including you. The hard part isn't loneliness — the hard part is having the energy for all the connection.

I traveled Egypt solo for three weeks. I had days where I wanted to be alone and I was. I had days where I wanted company and it appeared without me asking. The key wasn't luck — it was having a structure. A booked transfer from the airport. A licensed guide for the sites where history needs a voice. A hotel that was vetted and safe. A local number I could call if something went sideways.
