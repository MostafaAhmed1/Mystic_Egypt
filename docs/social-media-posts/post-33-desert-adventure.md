## Hook

## Caption
The Western Desert of Egypt is not what you expect when you sign up for a "desert safari." It's not sand dunes and camel rides and a sunset photo op. It's one of the most empty places on the planet.

We go out in convoy, well-equipped 4x4s with experienced desert drivers. We drive for hours and the only thing that changes is the sand — sometimes flat and hard, sometimes rolling and soft, sometimes scattered with black volcanic rock that looks like it came from another planet. We stop at oases where the water is warm and the palms are thick and the air smells like life against all odds. We sleep under stars that are so bright and so numerous they make you feel small in a way that's hard to describe.

We ate dinner around a fire one night and realized we hadn't heard a human voice that wasn't ours in 12 hours. No radio. No phone signal. No distant hum of a highway. Just wind, fire, and conversation. And the next morning, we watched the sun rise over white rock formations that have been shaped by wind over thousands of years. It didn't feel like a view. It felt like being let in on a secret.

That's what a real desert expedition feels like. Not a theme park version with set times and photo stops and a guide trying to upsell you on a necklace. It's time and space and silence, all of which are rarer than we admit.
