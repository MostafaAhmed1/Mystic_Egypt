## Hook
"The first time I stood in front of the Great Pyramid, I forgot to be impressed. It felt like a Tuesday."

## Caption
That happened to me on my first morning in Egypt. I had read every book, watched every documentary, and still stood there with a strange quiet that was almost disappointment. Not because it wasn't real — but because reality is always less dramatic than your imagination.

Except it wasn't. It was exactly what I wanted, and also more. I had a coffee the next morning at a place where the owner knew my name by the second day. I watched a sunset from the deck of a boat on the Nile and felt the whole weight of history make sense in one breath. I walked through Cairo's Islamic quarter and smelled the spices and heard the call to prayer from a minaret I had only seen in photographs.

This is the part no brochure shows you. Not the pyramid itself — that's just the start. The real Egypt is in the in-between moments. The stranger who refuses to take your money for directions. The guide who tells you a story that stops your mind from thinking for ten minutes. The food that's not on any restaurant menu you've seen before.
